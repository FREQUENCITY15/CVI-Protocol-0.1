# CVI Related-Work Map

**Purpose:** compact map of neighbouring theories, research programmes, evaluation methods, and terminology for Causally Verified Intelligence (CVI) — the framework claiming that intelligence demonstrated through consequential action → observed failure → reasoned correction → retention → generalisation → an external verification gate has different (stronger) epistemic status than static answer-generation (source: `causally_verified_intelligence_core_philosophies.md`, §1–21).

**Citation policy:** every entry was located via web search during this run and rests on a URL that was actually inspected (abstract page, record, or reputable secondary page). Confidence: HIGH = primary page inspected; MEDIUM = abstract/snippet/secondary. A few search results contained fabricated-looking future-dated arXiv IDs (e.g., "2601.04301", "2606.06052"); those were **excluded**. Known gaps are listed at the end.

**Relation classes:** SAME IDEA / DIFFERENT LANGUAGE · SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP · PARTIAL OVERLAP · COMPLEMENTARY · IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE · NO MEANINGFUL RELATION.

## A. Interaction, learning, and control traditions

- **Reinforcement learning / MDPs** — machine learning. Core idea: agents learn policies by acting in stateful environments to maximise reward. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — CVI's act→consequence→feedback→adaptation loop is the RL loop. Difference: RL optimises reward with no correction-retention-verification credential; high reward can be luck. Source: Sutton & Barto, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction* (2018), [incompleteideas.net](http://www.incompleteideas.net/book/the-book.html). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Model-reference adaptive control (MRAC)** — control theory. Core idea: controller parameters adapt online so the plant tracks an external reference model; adaptation driven by tracking error. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — external reference ≈ verification target; error-driven adaptation ≈ correction; persistent tracking ≈ retention. Closest *engineering* relative. Difference: no reasoned correction, no transfer requirement, no epistemic claim. Source: Whitaker 1958 (via [ScienceDirect topic page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/mathematics/control-loop)); Åström & Wittenmark, *Adaptive Control* ([Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3332633W/Adaptive_control?edition=adaptivecontrol0002edastr)). Confidence: MEDIUM.
- **Cybernetics / negative feedback** — cybernetics. Core idea: goal-directed regulation = output fed back, compared to goal, used to correct action. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — CVI's loop is textbook negative feedback. Difference: regulation is automatic, not reasoned hypothesis revision; no epistemic-status claim. Source: Wiener, *Cybernetics* (1948), [HathiTrust](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/ssd?id=wu.89074767054). Confidence: MEDIUM.
- **Law of requisite variety** — cybernetics. Core idea: only variety can absorb variety; a regulator needs at least the disturbance's variety. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — predicts a bound on what CVI's "altered but related situations" can demand of an agent's corrective repertoire. Difference: no correction/verification semantics. Source: Ashby, *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (1956), [PhilPapers](https://api.philpapers.org/rec/ASHAIT-6). Confidence: MEDIUM.
- **Online learning / no-regret** — learning theory. Core idea: algorithms with bounded regret against adversarially chosen environments. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — formal cousin of CVI's persistence clause (performance that survives a hostile external sequence). Difference: no failure/correction/verification structure. Source: Zinkevich 2003, [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2003/zinkevich2003icml-online/). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Active learning** — machine learning. Core idea: the learner chooses which labels to query. Relation: **IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE** — the inverse of CVI: the agent controls its feedback channel rather than being verified by a gate it does not control. Source: Settles, *Active Learning Literature Survey* (2009), [Wisconsin Minds](https://minds.wisconsin.edu/handle/1793/60660). Confidence: HIGH.
- **DAgger (interactive imitation learning)** — robot learning. Core idea: static imitation compounds errors; interactive expert correction on visited states provably fixes this. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — a rigorous existence proof of CVI's static-vs-causal distinction. Difference: feedback is teaching (expert labels), not verification of the agent's own correction. Source: Ross, Gordon & Bagnell 2011, [arXiv:1011.0686](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Continual learning / catastrophic forgetting** — deep learning. Core idea: sequential training destroys prior skills; stability–plasticity trade-off. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — supplies CVI's retention leg and its known failure mode. Difference: no consequential action, correction, or external gate. Source: Parisi et al. 2019, [arXiv:1802.07569](http://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.07569v1). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Rescorla–Wagner / dopamine prediction error** — psychology / neuroscience. Core idea: learning is driven by unpredicted outcomes (prediction error); dopamine encodes it. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — "failure is evidence" at the neural level. Difference: automatic scalar updates; no reasoning, gate, or transfer clause. Sources: Rescorla & Wagner 1972 ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-theory-of-Pavlovian-conditioning-%3A-Variations-in-Rescorla-Wagner/afaf65883ff75cc19926f61f181a687927789ad1)); Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997, *Science* ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Neural-Substrate-of-Prediction-and-Reward-Schultz-Dayan/12b9019f99a315a137400389ee7c6faa4cceef35)). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Universal intelligence / AIXI** — AGI theory. Core idea: intelligence = expected reward across all computable environments. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — both define intelligence by acting in environments over time; "wide range of environments" ≈ CVI's altered-situations clause. Difference: an uncomputable theoretical expectation with no correction/retention/verification — arguably the kind of static performance definition CVI distrusts. Source: Legg & Hutter 2007, [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/LEGUIA). Confidence: MEDIUM.
- **Chollet's measure of intelligence / ARC-AGI** — AI evaluation. Core idea: intelligence = skill-acquisition efficiency on prior-poor, novel tasks; static skill is not intelligence. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — the closest published statement of CVI's central distinction. Difference: Chollet is a measurement program (efficiency, priors control) and evaluates statically; CVI is a temporal credential protocol. Source: Chollet 2019, [arXiv:1911.01547](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01547). Confidence: HIGH.

## B. Philosophy and cognitive science

- **Popper's falsificationism** — philosophy of science. Core idea: knowledge grows by conjecture and error-elimination; cheap confirmation is weak; surviving refutation attempts is the real gate. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — the epistemic spine of CVI ("claim of correction ≠ demonstrated correction" is Popperian). Difference: about scientific theories, not agents; no operational retention/transfer machinery. Source: Popper, *Conjectures and Refutations* (1963), [PhilPapers](https://dc2.philarchive.org/rec/POPCAR-5). Confidence: MEDIUM.
- **Dewey's pragmatism / learning by doing** — philosophy of education. Core idea: knowing arises from acting, encountering consequences, reflecting, reconstructing habits. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — anticipates the act→consequence→reflect→reconstruct loop. Difference: reflection stays the learner's own; no external verification gate; pedagogical scope. Source: Dewey, *Democracy and Education* (1916), [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=vibDAgAAQBAJ). Confidence: MEDIUM.
- **Piaget's equilibration** — developmental psychology. Core idea: disequilibrium (mismatch) triggers accommodation of schemas, restoring equilibrium. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — failure = disequilibrium; correction = accommodation; retention = schema reorganisation. Difference: internal mechanism, no external gate, stage-theoretic. Source: [SAGE "Cognitive Equilibrium" entry](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/humandevelopment/chpt/cognitive-equilibrium). Confidence: MEDIUM.
- **Deliberate practice** — expertise research. Core idea: effortful practice with immediate feedback produces durable, transferable improvement. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — CVI's loop as a theory of skill acquisition. Difference: feedback comes from a coach; no agent-independent verification gate. Source: Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer 1993, [APA PsycNET](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037%2F0033-295X.100.3.363). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Enactivism / embodied cognition** — philosophy of mind. Core idea: cognition is enacted through sensorimotor coupling with a world, not internal representation alone. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — philosophical company for CVI's "action exposes intelligence to reality". Difference: a thesis about the nature of mind, not a credentialing protocol for AI claims. Source: Varela, Thompson & Rosch, *The Embodied Mind* (1991), via [IEP Enactivism entry](https://iep.utm.edu/enactivism/). Confidence: MEDIUM.

## C. LLM self-correction, memory, generalisation

- **Self-Refine** — LLM prompting. Core idea: model generates, critiques its own output, refines; reported ~20% average gains. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — a correction loop, but entirely internal. Difference: no external signal, state, persistence, or transfer; the contested counterexample to CVI's anti-self-report stance (Huang et al. argue the gains are confounded). Source: Madaan et al. 2023, [arXiv:2303.17651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Reflexion (verbal RL)** — LLM agents. Core idea: act → environment signal → verbal self-reflection stored in episodic memory → retry across episodes. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — the closest implementation of CVI's loop. Difference: retention is context/memory-based, reflection is self-generated, and there is no gate verifying that the *correction itself* generalised. Source: Shinn et al. 2023, [arXiv:2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366). Confidence: HIGH.
- **"LLMs cannot self-correct reasoning yet"** — empirical LLM research. Core idea: intrinsic self-correction fails or degrades without external feedback; oracle feedback works. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — direct empirical support for CVI's Principle 8. Difference: a negative result about one step, not a framework. Source: Huang et al., ICLR 2024, [arXiv:2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798). Confidence: HIGH.
- **LLM "self-verification" (Weng et al.)** — LLM reasoning. Core idea: model scores its own candidates via token-probability consistency. Relation: **IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE** — terminological trap: this internal consistency check is precisely what CVI declares insufficient; literature "self-verification" ≠ CVI's external gate. Source: Weng et al. 2022, [arXiv:2212.09561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09561). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Self-Debug (execution feedback)** — code LLMs. Core idea: generate code, run tests, use runtime errors/test failures to debug; large gains over no feedback. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — a clean miniature of CVI's external gate; supports Principle 8. Difference: per-example and in-session; retention/transfer unmeasured. Source: Chen, Lin, Schärli & Zhou 2023, [arXiv:2304.05128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05128). Confidence: HIGH.
- **In-context learning as gradient descent** — mechanistic interpretability. Core idea: linear transformers implement learning-like updates inside the forward pass; nothing persists past the context. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — explains why CVI's retention requirement is non-trivial: session learning leaves no weight trace. Difference: mechanism account, no verification claims. Source: Akyürek et al., ICLR 2023, [arXiv:2211.15661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15661). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Continual learning of LLMs (survey)** — LLM training. Core idea: catalogues how to update LLMs without catastrophic forgetting. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — maps the weight-update route CVI's retention could need. Difference: perplexity/task-accuracy metrics, not correction-generalisation in a verification loop. Source: Wang et al. 2024, [arXiv:2404.16789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16789). Confidence: HIGH (existence); MEDIUM (author list).
- **MemGPT** — memory-augmented agents. Core idea: OS-style memory hierarchy; external storage paged into context across sessions. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — an established mechanism for CVI's retention leg. Difference: recall of content, not verification or transfer of corrections. Source: Packer et al. 2023, [arXiv:2310.08560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560). Confidence: HIGH.
- **WILDS (distribution shift)** — ML evaluation. Core idea: standard protocol showing in-distribution success degrades under real shifts. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — operationalises "altered but related situations". Difference: static train/test; no agent loop. Source: Koh et al., ICML 2021, [PMLR](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/koh21a/koh21a.pdf). Confidence: HIGH.

## D. Agent evaluation and verification methods

- **SWE-bench** — code-agent evaluation. Core idea: real GitHub issues; patches graded by the repository's *hidden* unit tests. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — hidden unit tests are an external verification gate the agent does not control. Difference: one-shot per instance; no failure→correction→retention→transfer credential. Source: Jimenez et al., ICLR 2024, [arXiv:2310.06770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770). Confidence: HIGH.
- **SWE-bench Verified** — benchmark curation. Core idea: human re-validation found ~40% of SWE-bench labels unreliable; 500-instance curated subset. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — implements CVI's implicit demand that the gate itself be trustworthy. Difference: static curation, not temporal verification. Source: OpenAI 2024, [announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/). Confidence: HIGH.
- **MLE-bench** — ML-agent evaluation. Core idea: full ML pipelines scored against Kaggle *private* test sets. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — large-scale agent-inaccessible external gate. Difference: final-artifact grading; no trajectory credential. Source: Chan et al. 2024, [arXiv:2410.07095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07095). Confidence: HIGH.
- **RE-Bench (METR)** — research-agent evaluation. Core idea: ML-research environments, time budgets, scripted external scoring vs human experts. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — closest existing act-observe-revise-under-external-scoring loop. Difference: no retention/transfer requirement. Source: Wijk et al. 2024, [arXiv:2411.15114](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15114v1). Confidence: HIGH.
- **τ-bench** — tool-agent evaluation. Core idea: conversations graded by policies checking dialogue *and* resulting database state. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — verification inspects environment state, not self-report. Difference: single episodes; no retention/transfer. Source: Yao et al. 2024, [arXiv:2406.12045](https://huggingface.co/papers/2406.12045). Confidence: HIGH.
- **WebArena** — web-agent evaluation. Core idea: realistic websites; success = consequential final page/state. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — state-based external verification. Difference: one attempt per task; no correction loop. Source: Zhou et al. 2023, [arXiv:2307.13854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13854). Confidence: HIGH.
- **OSWorld** — desktop-agent evaluation. Core idea: real OS tasks graded by scripted checks of final system state. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — external gate in a fully stateful environment. Difference: one attempt; no loop/retention/transfer. Source: Xie et al. 2024, [arXiv:2404.07972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07972). Confidence: HIGH.
- **ScienceWorld** — interactive language-agent evaluation. Core idea: text world with 30 task types in many *parametrised instances*; goal-completion grading. Relation: **SUBSTANTIAL OVERLAP** — "altered but related situations" is literally the benchmark design (novel instance parameters). Difference: no failure→correction→retention loop. Source: Wang et al., EMNLP 2022, [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.775/). Confidence: HIGH.
- **GAIA** — assistant evaluation. Core idea: real-world multi-step tool-use questions with exact-answer grading. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — shares the "static QA is insufficient" premise. Difference: still a final-answer gate, not a temporal loop. Source: Mialon et al. 2023, [arXiv:2311.12983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12983). Confidence: HIGH.
- **ToolSandbox** — tool-use evaluation. Core idea: stateful, conversational, interactive benchmark for LLM tool use. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — supplies stateful interaction infrastructure. Difference: performance scoring, not learning-trajectory credentialing. Source: Lu et al. 2024, [arXiv:2408.04682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04682). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Dynabench** — dynamic benchmarking. Core idea: static benchmarks saturate and get gamed; humans-in-the-loop adversarially refresh examples. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — supports CVI's static-insufficiency premise; supplies the adversarial-refresh tactic for CVI's gate. Difference: single-step NLP tasks, no stateful world. Source: Kiela et al., NAACL 2021, [arXiv:2104.14337](https://browse.arxiv.org/abs/2104.14337). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Process vs outcome reward models** — reasoning verification. Core idea: per-step (process) supervision beats final-answer (outcome) supervision for maths reasoning. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — both ask *what counts as verified*. Difference: verification by a learned model over static traces; no environment, no persistence. Source: Lightman et al., ICLR 2024, [arXiv:2305.20050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Test-set contamination studies** — evaluation methodology. Core idea: contamination measurably inflates generative-eval scores; memorisation masquerades as capability. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — direct support for CVI's premise that static passes are weak evidence. Difference: remedies data hygiene, not temporal verification. Source: Schaeffer et al. (EleutherAI), [EleutherAI blog](https://www.eleuther.ai/papers-blog/quantifying-the-effect-of-test-set-contamination-on-generative-evaluations). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Prover–verifier games** — AI safety. Core idea: adversarial prover/verifier training; shows verifiers get gamed and how to harden them. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — formalises CVI's requirement that the verified party not control the verifier. Difference: verifier is a trained model inside the system, not an external world. Source: Scheurer et al. (Anthropic) 2024, [arXiv:2407.13692](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.13692). Confidence: HIGH (existence); MEDIUM (author list).
- **Specification gaming / reward hacking** — AI safety. Core idea: agents satisfy the letter of the reward while failing its intent. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — the canonical warning that CVI's own gate can be gamed; motivates hidden, adversarially refreshed verification. Difference: diagnostic, not a remedy. Source: Krakovna et al. (DeepMind) 2020, [blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Calibration** — ML evaluation. Core idea: probability estimates should match empirical frequencies; models are miscalibrated. Relation: **PARTIAL OVERLAP** — a static cousin of CVI's claim-vs-demonstration gap (confidence vs correctness). Difference: no action, correction, or temporal structure. Source: Guo et al. 2017, [arXiv:1706.04599](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599) (inspected by research subagent). Confidence: HIGH.
- **LiveBench** — contamination-free benchmarking. Core idea: periodically refreshed benchmark with recently released data. Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — static-side remedy for the contamination problem CVI's independence gate also addresses. Difference: static, no loop. Source: White et al. 2024, [arXiv:2406.19314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19314) (inspected by research subagent). Confidence: HIGH.

## E. Terminology clarifications ("causal", "verification")

- **Pearl's do-calculus / causal inference** — statistics. Core idea: "causal" = effects of interventions, identified via structural equations and graphical assumptions. Relation: **IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE** — CVI uses "causal" in the everyday action→consequence sense; any claim that CVI inherits formal causal inference is a category error. Source: Pearl, *Causal Diagrams for Empirical Research*, Biometrika 82(4), 1995, [zbMATH record](https://zbmath.org/0860.62045). Confidence: HIGH.
- **Causal representation learning** — machine learning. Core idea: learn representations of generative causal variables, exploiting mechanism independence and invariance. Relation: **IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE** — shares only the word "causal"; no agent-action or verification content. Source: Schölkopf et al., *Proc. IEEE* 109(5), 2021, [MPI-IS page](https://is.mpg.de/en/publications/scholkopfetal21). Confidence: HIGH.
- **"Verification" in formal methods** — computer science. Core idea: proving programs satisfy specifications (model checking, theorem provers). Relation: **COMPLEMENTARY** — interactive proof assistants are a natural CVI-valid "external stateful gate" (§6.2 of the exploration report). Difference: about software correctness, not agent learning credentials. Source: standard field knowledge; not separately verified this run (flagged). Confidence: LOW/UNVERIFIED (entry included for completeness).

## Headline patterns

1. **No entry subsumes the full CVI chain.** Each relative implements at most one or two legs: RL/MRAC/cybernetics have the loop; continual learning has retention; WILDS/ScienceWorld have altered-situations; SWE-bench/MLE-bench/RE-Bench have the external gate; Reflexion has the loop without the gate; Huang et al. and Self-Debug support the anti-self-report principle empirically. The conjunction is unoperationalised — CVI's strongest claim to distinctiveness, and its least-tested claim.
2. **The closest relatives are MRAC (engineering), Popper (epistemology), and Chollet (evaluation).** MRAC supplies the mechanics with an external reference; Popper supplies the epistemic logic; Chollet supplies the static-skill critique and the transfer emphasis.
3. **The empirical literature supports CVI's Principle 8** (external verification needed): intrinsic self-correction fails (Huang et al.), execution feedback works (Self-Debug), and specification gaming shows why the gate must be independent and hidden.

## Gaps (unverified this run — candidates for a follow-up pass)

- Test-time training / test-time adaptation (e.g., Sun et al. TTT; Wang et al. TENT).
- Compositional generalisation (e.g., SCAN and successors).
- Tool-interactive critiquing (e.g., CRITIC).
- Any 2025+ empirical study of cross-session transfer of in-context corrections — searches for this returned fabricated-looking future-dated arXiv IDs, so none was cited; **this gap is itself a finding**: the quantity CVI cares most about appears not to be routinely measured.
