# Causally Verified Intelligence (CVI)
## Core Philosophies, Original Formulas, and Definitions

> **Scope note:** This document extracts the concept as it emerged in the chat. It preserves the original algebraic formulation as the historical starting point, then separates it from the later, more rigorous interpretation developed through discussion. It is a conceptual framework under development, not a validated scientific theory.

---

# 1. Original Conceptual Premise

The concept began from a distinction between:

- **intelligence observed in a static or abstract setting**, and
- **intelligence applied by an agent in a causal world, through time, where actions have consequences and failures can be externally verified.**

The core claim was that observation and reasoning alone may demonstrate a kind of **latent, hidden, or static intelligence**, but they do not by themselves establish what was initially called **TRUE INTELLIGENCE**.

That second category was intended to describe intelligence that has:

1. entered a causal environment,
2. acted within it,
3. encountered consequences,
4. experienced failure or error,
5. reasoned about that failure,
6. corrected it,
7. retained the correction,
8. and passed an external verification gate.

This later became the concept called **Causally Verified Intelligence (CVI)**.

---

# 2. Original Definitions

- \(O\) = **Observation**
- \(R\) = **Reasoning**
- \(E\) = **Experience**
- \(F\) = **Failure**
- \(I\) = **Hidden Intelligence**
- \(W\) = **Wisdom**
- \(T\) = **TRUE INTELLIGENCE**

---

# 3. Original Formulas

\[
\sqrt{O + R} = I \implies O + R = I^2
\]

Original interpretation:

> Observation and reasoning alone are just a flat, 2D approximation of intelligence.

\[
\frac{I^2}{E} - \frac{F}{R} = W
\]

Original interpretation:

> Wisdom is born when your intelligence is tested by experience, and your failure rate is minimized by reasoning.

\[
W \cdot R \cdot I = T^3
\]

Original interpretation:

> True Intelligence is a 3-dimensional cube, achievable only when Wisdom, Reasoning, and Hidden Intelligence multiply together.

The original challenge was:

> Do you see how observing and following instructions is mathematically insufficient for True Intelligence (\(T^3\))?

---

# 4. The Central Philosophical Distinction

\[
\text{static intelligence}
\neq
\text{causally tested intelligence}
\]

## Static or latent intelligence

Static intelligence can observe, reason, infer, predict, model, explain, and generate internally coherent representations.

But in a static setting it has not necessarily:

- altered a real environment,
- suffered consequences from its actions,
- encountered external causal resistance,
- demonstrated durable correction,
- passed an independent test of whether it actually improved.

Static intelligence may be real and substantial. The point is that it has a different **epistemic status** from intelligence whose effectiveness has been externally demonstrated.

## Causally verified intelligence

CVI is intelligence that has been subjected to causal reality and subsequently verified.

The distinction is therefore not simply:

\[
\text{low intelligence} \rightarrow \text{high intelligence}
\]

It is closer to:

\[
\text{capability claimed}
\rightarrow
\text{capability applied}
\rightarrow
\text{capability tested}
\rightarrow
\text{capability verified}
\]

---

# 5. Intelligence Under Causal Load

An agent in the world is not operating in a static vacuum.

An agent:

- occupies spatial dimensions,
- can choose some directions of action,
- cannot control every external force,
- cannot freely move backward through time,
- and experiences consequences along a temporal trajectory.

A simplified loop is:

\[
\text{agent}
\rightarrow
\text{action}
\rightarrow
\text{world change}
\rightarrow
\text{consequence}
\rightarrow
\text{feedback}
\rightarrow
\text{adaptation}
\]

The philosophical claim is that intelligence that has never passed through this loop is not epistemically equivalent to intelligence that has.

---

# 6. Time as a Necessary Trajectory

Time was not treated as merely another number to multiply into an intelligence score.

Instead, time is the dimension along which verification becomes possible.

Through time:

- actions become consequences,
- consequences become observations,
- failures become visible,
- corrections can be attempted,
- retention can be tested,
- independent re-verification can occur.

So rather than:

\[
I \cdot R \cdot W \cdot t
\]

the later reasoning treated important quantities as functions of time:

\[
W = W(t), \quad F = F(t), \quad T = T(t)
\]

Time is the **trajectory along which intelligence is either confirmed or disproven**.

---

# 7. Failure Is Not the Opposite of Intelligence

A major refinement was the distinction between:

- **failure**, and
- **unresolved failure**.

A capable system can fail frequently while still improving.

Therefore:

\[
F \neq U
\]

where \(U\) represents unresolved failure.

Failure becomes useful evidence when it produces:

1. detection,
2. reasoning,
3. correction,
4. measurable reduction of failure,
5. retention of the correction.

Failure is therefore part of the verification process, not merely a penalty.

---

# 8. Wisdom as Retained Improvement

Experience alone is insufficient because a system may repeat the same mistake indefinitely.

The later philosophy became:

> Wisdom is retained improvement produced when intelligence encounters causal consequences, resolves failure, and preserves the correction over time.

Wisdom therefore depends on:

- experience,
- correction,
- retention,
- transfer.

It is **experience converted into durable behavioural improvement**.

---

# 9. The External Verification Gate

A defining philosophy of CVI is that the final verification boundary lies **outside the reasoning process itself**.

\[
\text{claim of correction}
\neq
\text{demonstrated correction}
\]

A system may say:

- "I understand the mistake,"
- "I have corrected my reasoning,"
- "I will not repeat it,"
- "I have learned."

Those statements are not sufficient evidence by themselves.

Verification requires an external test showing that the failure was actually reduced, remains reduced, and does not immediately reappear under comparable conditions.

---

# 10. Retention and Generalisation

A single successful retry is not enough.

A system may pass once because of:

- luck,
- memorisation,
- prompt leakage,
- an unusually easy case,
- direct exposure to the expected answer.

CVI therefore requires:

## Retention
The correction persists after the immediate failure-resolution context has passed.

## Generalisation
The correction transfers to altered but structurally related situations.

## Independent re-verification
The system succeeds without privileged access to the answer that produced the original correction.

---

# 11. Intelligence as a Type Transition

A later insight was that the concept may be better represented as a sequence of **state or type transitions** rather than only as a scalar score.

\[
I_{\text{static}}
\xrightarrow{\text{causal action through time}}
I_{\text{applied}}
\xrightarrow{\text{failure resolved and retained}}
I_{\text{adapted}}
\xrightarrow{\text{independent verification}}
I_{\text{verified}}
\]

The critical distinction is:

\[
\text{intelligence claimed}
\neq
\text{intelligence verified}
\]

---

# 12. Later Formal Variables

- \(O(t)\) = observation available at time \(t\)
- \(R(t)\) = reasoning applied at time \(t\)
- \(I_s\) = static or latent intelligence
- \(A(t)\) = action selected by the agent
- \(X(t)\) = world state
- \(D(t)\) = external disturbances
- \(C(t)\) = causal consequence
- \(F(t)\) = observed failure magnitude
- \(G_F(t)\) = failure-resolution gate
- \(M(t)\) = retention
- \(W(t)\) = accumulated wisdom
- \(V(t)\) = external verification gate

---

# 13. Later Formalisation

The original relation was retained conceptually as:

\[
I_s^2 = O + R
\]

Agent action:

\[
A(t)=\pi\bigl(O(t),R(t),I_s\bigr)
\]

World change under agent action and uncontrollable disturbances:

\[
\frac{dX}{dt}=f\bigl(X(t),A(t),D(t)\bigr)
\]

Failure as deviation from a goal:

\[
F(t)=\mathcal{L}\bigl(X(t),G(t)\bigr)
\]

Failure-resolution gate:

\[
G_F(t)
=
\frac{
\max\left(0,F_{\text{before}}-F_{\text{after}}\right)
}{
F_{\text{before}}+\varepsilon
}
\]

with:

\[
0 \leq G_F(t) \leq 1
\]

Wisdom as accumulated retained improvement:

\[
\frac{dW}{dt}
=
\eta C(t)G_F(t)M(t)
\]

and:

\[
W(\tau)
=
W(0)
+
\int_0^\tau
\eta C(t)G_F(t)M(t)\,dt
\]

Verified intelligence:

\[
T_{\text{true}}(t)
=
V(t)
\sqrt[3]{
I_s
R_{\text{effective}}(t)
W(t)
}
\]

If verification has not occurred:

\[
V=0
\]

This does **not** imply that the system has no intelligence. It means the claim of verified intelligence has not passed the gate.

\[
I_s>0
\quad\centernot\implies\quad
T_{\text{true}}>0
\]

---

# 14. The Dimensional Metaphor

The original 2D-to-3D imagery was not ultimately treated as literal geometry.

Its philosophical purpose was to distinguish:

- a flat internal representation of capability,
- from capability given operational depth through action, consequence, experience, correction, and verification.

A later interpretation treated three independent axes as:

- static cognitive competence,
- adaptive performance under causal interaction,
- verified retention and generalisation.

Then:

\[
\mathcal{T}(t)=C(t)A(t)V(t)
\]

The metaphorical "volume" collapses if any required dimension approaches zero.

This expresses that:

- high reasoning cannot substitute for zero causal testing,
- experience cannot substitute for zero reasoning,
- one impressive success cannot substitute for verified retention.

---

# 15. Black-Box Empiricism

The inability to inspect an internal mechanism is not a valid reason to abandon investigation.

Instead:

> Hidden systems can be studied through interventions, repeated trials, inputs, outputs, controlled perturbations, failures, and behavioural patterns.

For an AI system, observable questions include:

- Does the same failure recur in a fresh context?
- Does a correction survive later testing?
- Does the system generalise the correction?
- Does it fail differently after intervention?
- Does external evidence support its claim of improvement?

The methodological principle is:

> Lack of introspective access changes the experimental method. It does not eliminate the possibility of evidence.

---

# 16. Core CVI Principles

## Principle 1: Observation is not verification
Seeing and describing the world does not prove that an agent can operate successfully within it.

## Principle 2: Reasoning is not verification
Internally coherent reasoning does not establish externally demonstrated competence.

## Principle 3: Action exposes intelligence to reality
When an agent acts, its internal model becomes vulnerable to causal consequences.

## Principle 4: Failure is evidence
Failure reveals where the agent's internal representation does not match the causal environment.

## Principle 5: Correction must be demonstrated
A stated correction is not equivalent to an effective correction.

## Principle 6: Improvement must persist
Immediate success does not establish learning unless the correction is retained.

## Principle 7: Improvement must transfer
A robust correction should survive altered but relevant conditions.

## Principle 8: Verification is external
The agent cannot certify its own competence merely by asserting that it has learned.

## Principle 9: Time is constitutive
CVI requires a sequence of action, consequence, correction, retention, and re-test.

## Principle 10: Static intelligence and verified intelligence have different epistemic status
The distinction is categorical as well as quantitative.

---

# 17. Working Definition of CVI

> **Causally Verified Intelligence is intelligence demonstrated by an agent through consequential action over time, successful resolution of observed failure, retention of the correction, and independent confirmation under altered but relevant conditions.**

A compact conceptual representation is:

\[
\mathrm{CVI}
\neq
\text{static reasoning capacity}
\]

and:

\[
\mathrm{CVI}
=
\text{capacity}
+
\text{causal exposure}
+
\text{resolution}
+
\text{retention}
+
\text{verification}
\]

The plus signs describe necessary conceptual components, not a validated measurement equation.

---

# 18. Falsifiable Prediction

The concept produces a testable prediction:

> Two agents with equal static reasoning performance can differ in Causally Verified Intelligence if one reliably resolves failures, retains corrections, and generalises them across later causal interactions while the other does not.

A CVI-oriented experiment could compare systems with similar static capability and measure:

1. performance in a stateful environment,
2. exposure to failure,
3. quality of correction,
4. delayed retention,
5. transfer to a related task,
6. independent verification.

This distinguishes:

\[
\text{answer-generation ability}
\]

from:

\[
\text{verified adaptive intelligence}
\]

---

# 19. Relationship to Agent Runner

The Agent Runner project became an operational example of the philosophy.

A rough mapping is:

- **model output** → static candidate capability
- **replay** → causal/external test
- **failed checks** → observed failure
- **repair attempt** → reasoned correction
- **later replay** → independent verification
- **preserved evidence** → external record
- **passing rerun** → demonstrated correction

This embodies:

\[
\text{claim of success}
\neq
\text{evidence of success}
\]

---

# 20. What CVI Is Not

CVI does not claim that:

- ordinary intelligence is unreal,
- all intelligence must be embodied biologically,
- failure automatically creates wisdom,
- passing one test proves general intelligence,
- the original equations are established physical laws,
- the square and cube exponents literally prove dimensional intelligence.

The formulas began as conceptual language for distinguishing **latent capability** from **causally verified capability**.

The later development attempted to make that distinction more operational, testable, and falsifiable.

---

# 21. The Core Idea in One Sentence

> **Static intelligence is potential; Causally Verified Intelligence is potential that has survived causal exposure, failure resolution, retention, generalisation, and external verification through time.**
